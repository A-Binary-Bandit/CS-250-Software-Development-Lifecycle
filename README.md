# CS-250-Software-Development-Lifecycle
During this project, I took on the role of a Scrum Master, a Product Owner, and a member of the Development/Testing team. As each member of the Scrum team I worked my way through each of the stages of the Agile method of software development. The end product was for a fictional web company, "SNHU Travel", which was created over the class of 6 weeks

For a full summary of the project, please open and review the Sprint Retrospective document. The final product is contained within the .jar files in this repository. Journal documents recount the responsibities I took on within each role for the project, starting with the Scrum Master.

Some things I learned during this project were how to interpret user needs and implement them into a final product. By studying the user stories that are created, I can create an experience that not only appeals to the average user, but also fully fills their needs within the completed product application. When developing these apps, it's important to understand not just the user's needs, but how they will interact with the finished application. Creating an intuitive and easy to navigate UI is just as important as developing loosely couple code.

All of the tasks are important, and the bigger the project the bigger the team needs to be. Working with a team means constant open communication, without of which the product will not be as good as it could have been. Being able to provide not only constructive criticisms, but also solutions to problems significant in the creation of the product. Being supportive of your team keeps things running smoothly, and maintains a positive work environment where everyone feels valued and no one is made to feel inadequate or less useful, which will in turn increase productivitiy. Good team members work closely with one another and value each other's opinions. After all, it will take the whole team working together to create a complex and polished final application.
